# Blackthorn 404: The Unremembered

**Project codename:** BT404  
**Release target:** browser / installable PWA / GitHub Pages  
**Visual target:** dark, detailed 16-bit SNES-era presentation with modern controls and accessibility  
**Design rule:** an original work. No names, characters, maps, symbols, dialogue, audio, creatures, story beats, or extracted assets from commercial games.

---

## 1. Definitive name

# BLACKTHORN 404: THE UNREMEMBERED

The title binds the project to Universo 404, names the location that carries the campaign, and makes the central threat a concept rather than a conventional monster.

Tagline: **Some houses are haunted. Blackthorn is remembering you.**

---

## 2. Premise

In 2026, conservator **Elena Voss** inherits Blackthorn Manor after the unexplained disappearance of her aunt Mara. The legal papers are valid, the house is real, but no public record agrees on who built it, how old it is, or how many rooms it contains.

Inside the manor Elena finds objects that contain memories belonging to people from different centuries. Touching one does not show her a vision: it temporarily makes her live as the person whose memory was trapped inside it. Each recovered memory changes the architecture of Blackthorn Manor in the present.

The deeper truth is that Blackthorn is not a haunted house. It is a machine built across centuries to pin human memory to physical space. Something outside ordinary causality — **The Unremembered** — consumes links between people, events and places. Its victims do not merely die; the world reorganises itself as if they had never mattered.

Elena's family has spent generations turning architecture, testimony and ritual into an imperfect defence. Every recovered chapter strengthens the manor and also gives the entity a clearer route back to Elena.

## 4. Protagonists

### Elena Voss — 2026
Architectural conservator. Intelligent, sceptical and persistent rather than combat-trained. Elena's arc is about accepting that memory is not equivalent to truth. She grows from observer to active custodian, but the game never turns her into an invulnerable action hero.

### Nara Kesh — 1198 BCE
Surveyor and keeper of tide markers. Reads celestial and geological patterns. Starts with a bronze spear. First person known to describe a place that disappears from shared memory.

### Marcus Aelian — 395 CE
Civil registrar at a collapsing frontier administration. Starts with a short sword and wax tablets. His chapter weaponises the idea that a person can be erased by changing the record that proves they existed.

### Ysabel Thorne — 1349
Lay apothecary serving an isolated priory during epidemic quarantine. Starts with a wood axe and lantern. She notices that patients are not dying at the rate the graves suggest: some are simply becoming absent from everyone's recollection.

### Lucia Varetti — 1587
Printer, illustrator and clandestine anatomist. Starts with a dagger and hand crossbow. Learns to use printed geometry as repeatable Sigil logic.

### Eleanor Blackthorn — 1790
Engineer and first deliberate architect of the modern manor. Starts with a duelling sabre. Converts an older structure into a physical mnemonic engine.

### Elias Ward — 1917
Stretcher bearer and former schoolteacher. Starts with a trench club and limited revolver. His maps change when soldiers forget that a trench or person ever existed.

### Naomi Pike — 1956
Radio engineer. Starts with a heavy torch and emergency flare pistol. Discovers signals that contain memories no living broadcaster remembers recording.

### Theo Voss — 1987
Young restoration surveyor and Elena's father. Starts with a pry bar. Records Blackthorn on tape while the house edits itself between camera cuts.

## 5. Antagonists

### The Unremembered
Not a god and not an animal. It is an absence that behaves like predation. It cannot create; it removes connections until reality closes over the gap. It becomes visible only indirectly: missing doorways, inconsistent portraits, contradictory documents, people using pronouns for someone they cannot name.

### The Ash Archivist
A recurring human-shaped custodian assembled from burnt records. It believes forgetting is mercy. It appears across eras under different descriptions and becomes an elite pursuer in late game.

### The Witness Choir
Multiple human memories forced to occupy the same perceptual space. Their speech overlaps and contradicts itself. They are the principal agents that destabilise Perception.

### The House
Blackthorn Manor is not evil, but it is defensive, damaged, and willing to trap memories to survive. Its goals and Elena's are aligned only part of the time.

## 6. Mythology

### The Pale Interval
A conceptual space between remembered cause and remembered effect. The Unremembered occupies it. Rooms touched by the Interval retain geometry but lose meaning: a nursery with no record of children, a dining room with a place setting nobody can identify.

### Anchors
Physical objects that preserve enough context to resist erasure. An Anchor may be a letter, bone tool, photograph, census seal, architectural drawing or audio tape. Anchors launch historical chapters.

### Memory Spines
Eight major Anchors integrated into Blackthorn Manor. Each contains one historical chapter and one structural instruction for the house.

### The Palimpsest Engine
Not a literal machine with gears. It is the total system formed by foundations, room ratios, inscriptions, resonant cavities, documents and repeated human acts. Blackthorn Manor is its current form.

### The Three Substrates
- **Flesh:** body, repair, binding, decay.
- **Mind:** perception, attention, memory, misdirection.
- **Void:** distance, absence, seals, interruption.

Magic in the game is procedural ritual logic rather than fantasy spellcasting.

## 7. Campaign structure

### Prologue — The Letter Without a Sender
Elena arrives during a storm, restores power to the entrance wing, discovers an unsigned letter written in her own handwriting and opens the first sealed route. The prologue teaches movement, interaction, Perception and safe-room saving.

### Chapter II — Salt Under the Stars
Nara maps a city whose eastern quarter vanishes from communal memory each dawn. The player learns Echo tracking and long-reach spear combat. Boss: **The Salt Astronomer**.

### Chapter III — The Last Census
Marcus must verify residents whose names vanish from official tablets. Identity itself becomes a puzzle resource. Boss: **The Last Enumerator**.

### Chapter I — The Bell Without a Tongue
Ysabel investigates a priory in which the quarantine bell rings although its clapper has been removed. Sound reveals paths and enemies but attracts Hollow creatures. Boss: **The Bell Without a Tongue**.

### Chapter IV — The Anatomy of a Shadow
Lucia discovers that certain shadows remain after their owners are erased. Projected geometry becomes a puzzle language. Boss: **The Anatomist's Shadow**.

### Chapter V — The House Learns Your Name
Eleanor deliberately rebuilds the manor around a pre-existing impossible room. Rotating rooms and architectural alignment dominate. Boss: **The First Tenant**.

### Chapter VI — Mud Remembers
Elias follows battlefield trenches that exist only when remembered by at least two people. Ammunition is scarce; survival and route planning matter. Boss: **The Company Without Faces**.

### Chapter VII — Broadcast Eleven
Naomi isolates a frequency that causes listeners to remember lives they never lived. Audio alignment and signal triangulation dominate. Boss: **The Dead Frequency**.

### Chapter VIII — Tape Zero
Theo documents the manor restoration in 1987 and realises each tape has one frame recorded before the camera was switched on. False continuity, recorded clues and temporal room states dominate. Boss: **The Man in the Missing Frame**.

### Final — The Room Behind Memory
Elena enters the fully reconstructed Palimpsest Engine. The finale mixes real-time combat, Sigil composition, room-state puzzles, Perception management and silent decisions made from prior campaign behaviour.

## 9. PERCEPTION system

Three player resources:

- **Health:** physical damage. Reaches 0 = death / checkpoint recovery.
- **Perception:** 100 = coherent reality, 0 = maximum distortion. It is not a second health bar.
- **Stability:** 0–100 resistance buffer. Stability regenerates in safe rooms and through specific actions; while available it absorbs part of Perception drain.

### Perception loss sources
Darkness, close exposure to entities, forbidden documents, corrupt rooms, certain Sigils, repeated parries against Void attacks, and scripted revelations.

### Thresholds
- **76–100 / Clear:** normal rendering and reliable UI.
- **51–75 / Doubt:** false shadows, distant audio, subtle palette drift.
- **26–50 / Fracture:** false doors, room-label substitutions, harmless phantom enemies, text echoes.
- **11–25 / Breach:** intentionally unreliable HUD values, stronger distortion, duplicated silhouettes, fake pickups.
- **0–10 / Interval:** severe but playable distortions. Interaction prompts and accessibility-critical information remain truthful.

### Safety rules
Perception effects never close the browser, fake OS dialogs, delete or corrupt real saves, touch user files, imitate malware, or claim the device is damaged. All distortion remains visually inside the game frame.

### Accessibility controls
Perception FX intensity: Full / Reduced / Minimal. Separate toggles for screen shake, flashes and chromatic offset. Gameplay consequences remain even when visual effects are reduced.

## 10. SIGILS system

A Sigil is built from **VERB + SUBSTRATE + INTENSITY**. Recipes are discovered through play rather than a rune alphabet copied from another work.

### Verbs
BIND, MEND, REVEAL, WARD, SEVER, ECHO.

### Substrates
FLESH, MIND, VOID.

### Intensities
I, II, III. Higher intensity increases cost, cooldown and side effects.

### Twelve campaign Sigils
1. BIND + VOID + I — Root an enemy or hold a moving mechanism.
2. WARD + MIND + I — Reduce Perception drain briefly.
3. MEND + FLESH + I — Slow health recovery with Stability cost.
4. REVEAL + MIND + I — Show false/real distinction in nearby objects.
5. SEVER + FLESH + I — Bleed vulnerable organic enemies.
6. ECHO + MIND + I — Replay the last meaningful room sound.
7. WARD + VOID + II — Create a short projectile barrier.
8. BIND + FLESH + II — Immobilise a wounded elite.
9. REVEAL + VOID + II — Expose hidden architecture / Interval seams.
10. MEND + MIND + II — Restore Perception but lower Stability ceiling temporarily.
11. SEVER + VOID + III — Break a major corruption link; boss/puzzle use.
12. ECHO + VOID + III — Temporarily overlay a historical room state onto the present.

The vertical slice implements BIND + VOID + I and WARD + MIND + I as representative systems.

## 14. Bosses

1. **The Salt Astronomer** — a moving boundary rather than a creature; three phases change walkable terrain.
2. **The Last Enumerator** — rewrites target identities; the player must protect/restore specific records.
3. **The Bell Without a Tongue** — invisible while silent; attacks reveal it through room vibration. Phase III requires deliberately ringing the impossible bell.

5. **The First Tenant** — inhabits rooms rather than a body; the player fights by reconfiguring architecture.
6. **The Company Without Faces** — formation boss built from mutually remembered soldiers; breaking witness links changes attacks.
7. **The Dead Frequency** — signal entity fought by tuning, movement and short combat windows.
8. **The Man in the Missing Frame** — exists only between recorded states; player uses playback continuity to expose it.
9. **The Unremembered** — final encounter. No giant-monster solution: combat, Sigils, room reconstruction, Perception management and previous decisions all matter.

All bosses have at least three phases, telegraphed attacks, an discoverable weakness, environment interaction, unique audio state and a narrative reward.

## 15. Endings

### Ending A — The Custodian
Elena repairs the Palimpsest Engine and accepts permanent stewardship of Blackthorn. The historical memories survive, but the house remains a necessary prison. This is the standard ending.

### Ending B — The Quiet House
Elena destroys enough Anchors to starve the Unremembered locally. The manor becomes ordinary and safe, but the people carried by those memories become unrecoverable. The final scene contains gaps in Elena's own family photographs.

### Ending C — The Remembered / True Ending
Requirements: all eight Memory Spines resolved, key contradictory documents retained, four hidden witnesses saved, REVEAL + VOID + II and ECHO + VOID + III learned, at least ten major secrets found, and no Anchor deliberately destroyed.

Elena refuses both imprisonment and erasure. She restructures the Engine so memories exist redundantly across people, objects and architecture. The Unremembered loses a single point of failure to consume. Blackthorn survives as an archive rather than a prison, but Elena must accept that some recovered histories remain contradictory.

The nine campaign bosses are governed by separate `BOSS_PROFILES`. ## Phase 11 continuity lock

The evidence archive is now the canonical continuity layer for the campaign. It contains 87 recoverable entries (40 documents, 20 notes, 12 recordings/transcripts and 15 counter-memories). The story should continue to obey these truths:

- The Unremembered primarily erases **relationships and distinctions**; factual gaps are a consequence.
- Contradictory accounts may both contain valid relational evidence. Forcing one official version can strengthen erasure.
- Blackthorn Manor is an intentional mnemonic structure, not merely a haunted house. Eleanor Blackthorn chose in 1790 to build around impossible geometry she did not create.
- The thorn-star / three-pronged geometry predates the Blackthorn family and appears in Asterion and Vespera.
- “MARA” is a recurring collapsed name-shape across unrelated erased people, not proof of one immortal character.
- Naomi Pike's Frequency Eleven depends on relationships between listeners rather than conventional transmitter location.
- Theo Voss deliberately preserves impossible footage; Tape Zero contains a future Elena warning that should not exist in 1987.
- Elena's final choices remain unresolved until Phase 12. Lore completion may contribute to the True Ending but must not silently override those choices.

## Phase 12 Canon — Endings

Blackthorn has **three authored endings**, all resolved from state already created during play. The player is never shown an ending-selection menu.

### Normal — THE HOUSE OF WITNESSES
Blackthorn remains an archive that preserves disagreement without requiring a single canonical history. Elena becomes a witness, not a permanent warden.

### Dark — THE NINTH TENANT
At least two final choices favor collapse, erasure or ownership. The house becomes perfectly consistent at the cost of human distinction, and Elena occupies the relational role the old mechanism required.

### True — NO ONE REMEMBERS ALONE
Canonical completionist resolution. Elena must preserve both witness accounts, keep Theo's warning, release the eight historical lives, recover all fifteen counter-memories and nine key evidence records, and master the complete three-school Sigil grammar. Blackthorn's mnemonic engine is dismantled without destroying evidence. The recurring MARA index resolves as many erased people collapsed into one empty documentary shape, not one immortal individual.

### Continuity rule for Phase 13+
`ending`, `endingsSeen[]` and `endingStats{}` are persistent schema-11 state and are the authorized inputs for New Game+. Phase 13 must not retroactively rewrite which ending was earned.

## PHASE 13 CANON — NEW GAME+ / THE SECOND READING

New Game+ is canonical meta-memory, not time travel and not a literal looping universe. The player has completed one authored reading of Blackthorn; the second cycle represents Elena/player knowledge leaking into how the mnemonic structure is interpreted.

### Rules that must remain true

1. NG+ resets campaign progression. No chapter, boss, key, puzzle or final choice is considered complete because it happened in a prior cycle.
2. `endingsSeen[]`, achievements, legacy evidence and cycle history are meta-state and may persist.
3. Legacy evidence is readable context only. It does not satisfy current-cycle True Ending evidence requirements until rediscovered.
4. The prior ending changes the texture of the second reading, not the canonical identity of characters or history.
5. Ending legacies are deliberately modest and begin at Sigil Intensity I; the Phase 5 Lattice remains meaningful.
6. Refracted Memories are NG+-exclusive counter-perspectives. They are not required for a first-run ending and must never become mandatory chapter keys.
7. Normal, Dark and True endings remain mutually incompatible authored outcomes. Seeing all three means the meta-reader remembers three versions; it does not collapse them into one canonical ending.
8. Enemy scaling is modest and capped. NG+ should feel sharper, not become a separate hard-mode balance problem.

## Phase 14 — UX / Mobile / PWA contract

Blackthorn 404 must remain fully playable in portrait and landscape on modern mobile browsers, including devices with display cutouts and home indicators. Safe areas are part of the layout contract, not decorative padding.

Touch interaction uses a minimum target around 44 px in the validated phone layouts. The player can choose always-visible, automatic or hidden touch controls, enlarge targets and invert the physical layout for left-handed use. These options do not alter combat timing or input semantics.

Landscape on coarse-pointer devices uses a dedicated two-zone presentation: game screen on the left, controls on the right. Portrait remains supported and receives only a brief non-blocking suggestion that landscape offers more combat space.

Fullscreen is progressive enhancement. If the platform exposes the Fullscreen API it is used; otherwise Blackthorn enters Focus Mode, which removes surrounding chrome and fits the game to the dynamic viewport without pretending to control the operating system.

Performance settings are cosmetic-only. Battery Saver may disable grain, scanlines, expensive blending and backdrop blur but must never remove gameplay information, enemies, puzzle state or actual PERCEPTION mechanics.

The PWA shell is versioned independently of saves. Navigation prefers the network when online so a new GitHub Pages release is not hidden behind stale HTML; same-origin static assets remain cache-friendly; activation deletes older `blackthorn404-*` caches. Offline fallback is the cached application shell. No account, telemetry, CDN, private API or server state is introduced.


## Phase 15 — Balance lock

The campaign balance pass keeps save schema 12 and preserves all narrative gates. Stamina uses post-action recovery delay so fast weapons can no longer sustain infinite attacks through passive regeneration. Safe thresholds are enemy-free by definition. Historical encounter density is flattened early and selectively reinforced in later memories, while enemy stat growth carries the long-term difficulty curve. Boss Phase III focus windows are tuned to remain solvable with slow historical weapons. PERCEPTION pressure is slightly smoother and NG+ scaling is capped at +15% HP, +12% damage and +5% speed. These values are centralized in `BALANCE` in `js/data.js` and are release-gated by `tools/balance_check.py`.
